Steroid Hormone Synthesis

One starting molecule, one shared trunk, three branches. Every hormone on this page is cholesterol with a few carbons hydroxylated, oxidized, or removed — and every disease on this page is one of those steps failing. Learn the map as plumbing and the diseases stop being a list to memorize: whatever sits above the block accumulates and gets diverted, whatever sits below it disappears.

What this session asks of you

The seven objectives for this session

1. Why carbon numbering matters → the numbering is the map. 2. The pathways and their locations → the pathway, by zone. 3. The one sentence version
Cholesterol (27 carbons) is cut down to pregnenolone (21 carbons), and from there three branches diverge: keep 21 carbons and you get mineralocorticoids and glucocorticoids; cut the side chain to 19 carbons and you get androgens; remove one more carbon and aromatize the A ring to 18 carbons and you get estrogens.
The habit worth building now

When you meet any steroid disorder, ask three questions in this order:

  • Is cortisol low? If so ACTH is high and the gland is hyperplastic.
  • Is there salt wasting or hypertension? That is the mineralocorticoid arm.
  • Are androgens high or low? That is the sex-steroid arm.

Those three answers name the enzyme without your having to recall a list.

OrientThe molecule, its carbons, and the cast of enzymes

The numbering is the map, not a decoration

Objective 1 asks you to explain to a colleague why skipping the carbon numbering is a mistake. Here is the argument.

The point in one line

Almost every enzyme in steroidogenesis is named after the carbon it acts on. If the numbers mean nothing to you, the enzyme names are arbitrary syllables and the pathway becomes rote. If the numbers mean something, the name of the enzyme tells you the reaction, and the reaction tells you what accumulates when it fails.

Read the name, predict the chemistry. Every one of these is a carbon position doing exactly what it says.

Say this to your colleague

You cannot tell 17α-hydroxylase from 17,20-lyase without the numbers, and those two are the same protein doing different jobs. You cannot explain why losing C21 hydroxylation wrecks both cortisol and aldosterone while losing C11 hydroxylation causes hypertension, without knowing where those carbons sit in the sequence. The numbering is not nomenclature — it is the mechanism written down.

C21 — pregnanes
Progesterone, 11-deoxycorticosterone, corticosterone, aldosterone, 11-deoxycortisol, cortisol. The full side chain is still attached.
C19 — androstanes
DHEA, androstenedione, testosterone, dihydrotestosterone. The 17,20-lyase step removed the side chain.
C18 — estranes
Estrone, estradiol. Aromatase removed the C19 methyl and made ring A aromatic.

The cast: cytochrome P450s and dehydrogenases

Two chemical families, two subcellular addresses. Knowing which is which explains why the intermediates shuttle in and out of the mitochondrion.

Cytochrome P450s — the hydroxylases
Heme mono-oxygenases that insert an oxygen atom, consuming O₂ and NADPH. Some are mitochondrial (CYP11A1, CYP11B1, CYP11B2), some sit in the smooth ER (CYP17A1, CYP21A2, CYP19A1) — which is why intermediates shuttle in and out of the mitochondrion. Every enzyme whose name contains "hydroxylase," "desmolase," "lyase," or "aromatase" is a P450.
Hydroxysteroid dehydrogenases
Not P450s. They move a hydrogen using NAD(P)⁺/NAD(P)H, interconverting a hydroxyl group and a ketone. 3β-HSD and 17β-HSD are the two you need. They are reversible in principle, so tissue expression of the isoform decides the direction.

Two naming points that catch people out

The slides write the 21-hydroxylase gene as CYP21A1; the functional gene is CYP21A2, sitting beside the non-functional pseudogene CYP21A1P — which is why most CAH alleles arise by gene conversion or deletion rather than point mutation. Second: aldosterone synthase (CYP11B2) and 11β-hydroxylase (CYP11B1) are separate genes that both carry 11β-hydroxylase activity. Hold on to that and the chimeric-gene disease later will make sense.

The whole map on one grid

Three columns, three products. Read left to right for the branch, top to bottom for the sequence.

Original diagram, drawn from the pathway presented in the session. The vertical axis is the sequence, the horizontal axis is the branch point: steps to the right modify C17, steps downward add oxygen at C21, C11, or C18. Two facts to carry away — 21-hydroxylase serves both the left and middle columns, which is why its loss is the most damaging single deficiency, and 17α-hydroxylase is the gate to both the middle and right columns, which is why its loss removes cortisol and all sex steroids while leaving the left column overdriven.

Trace three paths out loud before moving on

To aldosterone: cholesterol → pregnenolone → progesterone → 11-deoxycorticosterone → corticosterone → 18-hydroxycorticosterone → aldosterone. To cortisol: cholesterol → pregnenolone → 17-OH-pregnenolone → 17-OH-progesterone → 11-deoxycortisol → cortisol. To testosterone: cholesterol → pregnenolone → 17-OH-pregnenolone → DHEA → androstenedione → testosterone, then 5α-reductase to DHT or aromatase to estradiol in the periphery.

Why each zone makes only its own hormone

Same genome in every cell of the cortex. What differs is which steroidogenic enzymes are transcribed — and that is the whole explanation.

Zona glomerulosa — 10–15%
Aldosterone. Expresses CYP11B2 (aldosterone synthase) and does not express CYP17A1. With no 17α-hydroxylation available, everything is forced down the mineralocorticoid branch. Driven by angiotensin II and potassium, not primarily by ACTH.
Zona fasciculata — 70–80%
Cortisol. Expresses CYP17A1, plus CYP21A2 and CYP11B1, but not cytochrome b5 — so it performs 17α-hydroxylation without the subsequent lyase step. Driven by ACTH, and the largest zone.
Zona reticularis — 10–15%
DHEA and androstenedione. Expresses CYP17A1 with cytochrome b5, so the lyase activity runs. Carries receptors for ACTH, LH, and FSH, all of which raise cAMP.
A mnemonic, and what it hides

Salt, sugar, sex — the deeper you go, the sweeter it gets. What it does not capture is the mechanism: the difference between the zones is enzyme expression, not anatomy. The medulla is a separate story — neural crest in origin, chromaffin cells making catecholamines — and appears on the adrenal disorders page.

Adrenal androgens are weak
DHEA and androstenedione are weak; potency comes from peripheral and gonadal conversion to testosterone and DHT. They dominate androgen exposure before puberty in both sexes; afterwards the gonads take over in males, while the adrenal remains a major androgen source in females — which is why adrenal androgen excess is a bigger clinical problem in girls and women.
Why the fetus is the vulnerable one
Excess adrenal androgen matters far more to 46,XX development in utero than to 46,XY: an XY fetus is already awash in testicular testosterone, whereas an XX fetus has a bipotential external genital anlage that responds to it. That asymmetry is the whole of objective 6.

One gene, one polypeptide, two activities

Objective 3 asks you to puzzle over this deliberately. It rewards the effort, because the answer explains the zonation you just learned.

The apparent paradox

CYP17A1 is one gene encoding one polypeptide with two catalytic activities: 17α-hydroxylase and 17,20-lyase. Adrenal androgen synthesis needs both; cortisol synthesis needs only the first. So how does one protein, present in both the fasciculata and the reticularis, run both reactions in one zone and only one in the other?

Original diagram. Cytochrome b5 is the switch — an allosteric partner that promotes the electron transfer the lyase reaction needs. Where b5 is expressed (zona reticularis) CYP17A1 carries on to a C19 androgen; where it is not (zona fasciculata) the reaction stops at 17-hydroxyprogesterone and 21- and 11β-hydroxylase take it to cortisol instead.

The resolution, stated plainly
The two activities are separably regulated even though they are inseparably encoded. 17α-hydroxylase runs wherever CYP17A1 is expressed; 17,20-lyase additionally requires cytochrome b5. Control the partner and you control which of the two reactions the cell performs.
Why this is worth the effort
It explains adrenarche — the rise in adrenal androgens at age 6 to 8, which tracks maturation of the zona reticularis and its b5 expression rather than any change in ACTH. And it explains why CYP17A1 deficiency takes out cortisol and all sex steroids together while leaving the mineralocorticoid arm over-supplied.
RegulateWhat turns each branch on, and on what timescale

Two separate control systems in one gland

Original diagram. Two branches, two second messengers: cAMP on the left, calcium on the right. Keeping them separate is what lets you predict which hormone is lost in which disease — and it is exactly this separation that glucocorticoid-remediable aldosteronism destroys.

ACTH acts on two timescales
  • Short term (minutes) — cAMP/PKA mobilizes StAR, moving cholesterol into the mitochondrion. This is where the rate-limiting step is regulated.
  • Long term (hours to days) — cAMP-responsive transcription factors induce StAR and the steroidogenic enzymes themselves, and the cortex grows.

That second mechanism is why chronically high ACTH produces hyperplasia — the "H" in congenital adrenal hyperplasia.

What drives ACTH
Diurnal rhythm — early-morning peak, midnight trough, which is the basis of the late-night salivary cortisol test on the adrenal page. And stress — illness, injury, surgery, hypoglycemia. Cortisol feeds back at both pituitary and hypothalamus, which is why exogenous glucocorticoid suppresses the whole axis.
Sex steroid production — the two-cell model

Ovary: LH drives the theca cell, which has CYP17A1 and makes androstenedione; that crosses to the granulosa cell, where FSH drives aromatase and it becomes estradiol. Neither cell can do the whole job alone. Testis: LH drives the Leydig cell to make testosterone, converted peripherally to DHT by 5α-reductase or estradiol by aromatase.

Glucocorticoid-remediable aldosteronism: building the "design flaw" argument

Objective 4 asks you to construct a rationale, not to recite a fact. Here is the chain of reasoning, one link at a time.

Original diagram. The reciprocal products of the same crossover have wildly different consequences. Putting a small hormone system under a large drive is a catastrophe; putting a large system under a small additional drive changes nothing measurable.

The argument, link by link

1. CYP11B2 arose from CYP11B1 by gene duplication — a dedicated aldosterone synthase, separately regulated from cortisol synthesis, was an evolutionary gain. 2. But the duplicates were left side by side on chromosome 8q24.3, ~95% identical in their coding regions. 3. Meiotic recombination pairs by sequence homology — it has no concept of gene identity, so CYP11B1 on one chromosome can pair with CYP11B2 on the other. 4. A crossover in that misaligned pairing yields a chimeric gene: the promoter of one fused to the coding sequence of the other. 5. The chimera carrying the CYP11B1 promoter with CYP11B2 coding sequence puts aldosterone synthase under ACTH control, expressed in the zona fasciculata. Disease. So the flaw is structural, not mutational — nothing was damaged and no base mis-copied; the arrangement of the genome itself makes the error recurrent. And the genome does it again: CYP21A2 sits beside its pseudogene CYP21A1P in the same arrangement, which is why most CAH alleles arise by gene conversion or deletion. Both major disorders in this session share one root cause — near-identical sequences kept in tandem.

What the name "glucocorticoid-remediable" is telling you

Objective 5: dissect the etymology, then the pathophysiology. The name is unusually informative — it names the treatment, not the lesion.

The etymology, word by word
Hyper-aldosteron-ism — too much aldosterone. Remediable — able to be remedied. Glucocorticoid — the drug class that remedies it. So: hyperaldosteronism that a glucocorticoid fixes. The trap is that the name sounds like a glucocorticoid deficiency state; it is not — the glucocorticoid is given as an ACTH suppressant, not as replacement. Also called familial hyperaldosteronism type 1, and autosomal dominant.
The pathophysiology in five steps
1. The chimeric gene expresses aldosterone synthase activity in the zona fasciculata, where it does not belong. 2. It is transcribed from the CYP11B1 promoter, so it answers to ACTH. 3. Aldosterone production therefore follows the ACTH rhythm rather than renin, angiotensin II, or potassium. 4. Aldosterone excess → sodium retention → volume expansion → hypertension, with hypokalemia and suppressed renin (volume expansion switches the renin–angiotensin system off). 5. Give dexamethasone: ACTH is suppressed, the abnormal drive is removed, and aldosterone normalizes. The response is not just treatment — it is the diagnostic demonstration.
Recognizing it clinically, and how it is confirmed

Suspect it in early-onset hypertension with a family history of hypertension or hemorrhagic stroke before age 40, especially with hypokalemia and a raised aldosterone-to-renin ratio. Because the fasciculata is now hydroxylating cortisol at C18, patients excrete the characteristic hybrid steroids 18-oxocortisol and 18-hydroxycortisol. Confirm with genetic testing for the chimeric gene. Treat with a low-dose glucocorticoid to suppress ACTH, a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist, or both. It is rare, but it is on the syllabus because the mechanism can be derived from first principles.

From a missing enzyme to a virilized XX fetus

Objective 6 asks you to formulate the mechanism. Six steps, each one following from the last.

Original diagram. The feedback arm makes this self-amplifying: missing cortisol raises ACTH, and higher ACTH pushes still more substrate into a pathway whose only open exit is the androgen branch. That is why an untreated infant does not simply have low cortisol — they have enormous androgen production.

The mechanism in six steps

1. 21-hydroxylase serves both arms — 17-OH-progesterone → 11-deoxycortisol, and progesterone → 11-deoxycorticosterone. 2. Lose it and cortisol and aldosterone both fall. 3. Low cortisol removes negative feedback, so ACTH rises — mobilizing cholesterol acutely and enlarging the cortex chronically (the hyperplasia of the name). 4. Substrate piles up above the block, so 17-OH-progesterone rises steeply. 5. The only route still open is 17,20-lyase → DHEA and androstenedione → peripherally to testosterone and DHT. 6. A 46,XX fetus has a bipotential external genital anlage that responds to androgen. Exposure before ~12 weeks produces labioscrotal fusion and a urogenital sinus; exposure after ~12–14 weeks, once those structures have formed, produces clitoromegaly without fusion.

Why the internal structures are unaffected

The affected fetus has normal ovaries, uterus, and fallopian tubes. Adrenal androgen cannot regress the Müllerian ducts — that needs AMH from Sertoli cells, and there are none — nor stabilize the Wolffian ducts, which need high local testosterone from an adjacent testis. So the virilization is external only, which is why these children are fertile, raised female, and surgically correctable. Clinical detail on the DSD page.

Classic and non-classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia

Objective 7. One gene, a spectrum of residual enzyme activity, and a presentation that tracks that residue almost linearly.

The organizing idea

The three forms are not three diseases. They are one enzyme deficiency at three severities. Ask how much 21-hydroxylase activity is left and everything else follows: near-zero activity loses cortisol and aldosterone; a little activity keeps aldosterone but not cortisol; a lot of activity keeps both under basal conditions and only shows up as androgen excess later in life.

21-hydroxylase deficiency accounts for roughly 90–95% of all congenital adrenal hyperplasia.

How each is found
Classic: the newborn screen measures 17-hydroxyprogesterone, which is how an affected 46,XY infant — normal-appearing at birth, and otherwise destined to present in shock — is caught before the crisis. Non-classic: an early-morning, follicular-phase 17-OHP, with an ACTH (cosyntropin) stimulation test if borderline. Genotyping confirms.
How each is treated
Classic: hydrocortisone, plus fludrocortisone and sodium chloride for the mineralocorticoid arm, plus stress dosing during illness. Prevent crisis while normalizing growth and androgens — over-treating stunts growth, under-treating advances bone age. Non-classic: often no treatment; glucocorticoid only for symptomatic androgen excess or subfertility.
The other blocks, in one line each

11β-hydroxylase — virilizing and hypertensive, because DOC accumulates. 17α-hydroxylase/17,20-lyase — no cortisol and no sex steroids, but DOC and corticosterone accumulate: hypertension with absent puberty and an undervirilized 46,XY infant. 3β-HSD — the one form in which both sexes can have atypical genitalia. Retrieval prompts

Answer out loud before opening each one.

Position in the pathway. 21-hydroxylase sits on the mineralocorticoid and the glucocorticoid arms but not on the androgen arm, so its loss removes aldosterone and cortisol while leaving the androgen exit open for the accumulating precursor — salt wasting plus virilization. 17α-hydroxylase sits at the gate to the glucocorticoid and androgen arms, so its loss removes cortisol and all sex steroids while diverting everything into the mineralocorticoid arm — DOC and corticosterone accumulate, giving hypertension with hypokalemia and no virilization.

Cytochrome b5. The 17α-hydroxylase reaction runs wherever CYP17A1 is expressed. The 17,20-lyase reaction additionally requires cytochrome b5 as an allosteric partner promoting electron transfer. b5 is expressed in the zona reticularis and not the zona fasciculata, so the reticularis makes androgens while the fasciculata stops at 17-hydroxyprogesterone and goes on to cortisol. The genetics are inseparable; the regulation is not.

Scale. Cortisol output is of the order of 10–20 mg per day; aldosterone output is of the order of 0.05–0.2 mg per day — a difference of roughly two orders of magnitude. Adding a little angiotensin-II-driven 11β-hydroxylase to an already large cortisol system is undetectable. Putting the tiny aldosterone system under the large, tonic ACTH drive transforms it. Same crossover, same two products, opposite clinical significance.

Non-classic 21-hydroxylase deficiency, which mimics PCOS. At birth she had normal genitalia and a normal newborn screen, because 20–50% residual enzyme activity is enough for normal cortisol and aldosterone under basal conditions and enough to keep fetal androgen exposure below the threshold for virilization. Confirm with an early-morning follicular-phase 17-OHP, and use an ACTH stimulation test if the value is equivocal.
