How to use a page like this

Reading these tables feels productive and mostly is not. Cover a column and generate it — say the answers before you look. The moment you find yourself nodding along, you have stopped learning and started recognizing. The recall prompts at the bottom of each section are there for exactly that reason.

Pairs that get confused

Each of these is a pair where the wrong answer is the other member of the pair. Learn the discriminator, not the pair.

PairThe discriminator
Hyperthyroidism vs thyrotoxicosisThyrotoxicosis is excess hormone from any source; hyperthyroidism means the gland is overproducing. A thyroiditis causes the first without the second.
Graves disease vs exogenous thyroid hormoneUptake scan — diffusely increased vs absent. Graves also has a goiter and may have eye disease.
Subacute granulomatous vs subacute lymphocytic thyroiditisPain. Granulomatous (de Quervain) hurts; lymphocytic does not, and is classically postpartum.
Riedel thyroiditis vs anaplastic carcinomaBoth are hard, fixed masses in an older patient. Tissue diagnosis — you cannot settle it clinically, and one is lethal.
Follicular adenoma vs follicular carcinomaCapsular invasion, which sits at the edge of the lesion — so FNA often cannot call it and excision is needed.
Papillary vs follicular carcinoma on FNAPapillary has nuclear features — Orphan Annie eye nuclei, psammoma bodies — so cytology works. Follicular does not.
Sheehan syndrome vs pituitary apoplexySpeed and mechanism. Sheehan is postpartum ischemic necrosis presenting insidiously; apoplexy is acute hemorrhage into an adenoma with a thunderclap headache.
Prolactinoma vs stalk effectMagnitude. Prolactin correlates with adenoma size, so a very high prolactin favors a prolactinoma; a moderate rise beside a non-prolactin-secreting mass is stalk effect.
Craniopharyngioma vs Rathke cleft cystBoth from Rathke pouch. The cyst is benign and often incidental; the craniopharyngioma grows, compresses the chiasm, and causes hypopituitarism.
Gigantism vs acromegalyEpiphyseal plates. Open → gigantism; closed → acromegaly. Same hormone, same tumor.
AVP deficiency vs AVP resistanceThe response to desmopressin. Clinically identical otherwise.
Bitemporal vs homonymous hemianopsiaWhere the lesion sits relative to the chiasm. At it → bitemporal. Behind it → homonymous. In front of it → one eye only.
Dawn phenomenon vs Somogyi effectThe overnight glucose. Drifting up vs dipping low then rebounding. The morning value looks identical.
DKA vs HHSHow much insulin is left. None → lipolysis and ketones. A little → enough to suppress lipolysis, not enough for glucose uptake.
T1DM vs T2DMAntibodies plus C-peptide, read alongside phenotype, age, and family history. No single feature is decisive.
Cushing disease vs Nelson syndromeWhether cortisol is high. Nelson follows adrenalectomy — very high ACTH with hyperpigmentation and mass effect, but no hypercortisolism.

GoiterImpaired hormone synthesis → TSH rises → hypertrophy and hyperplasia.
Adrenergic look of hyperthyroidismThyroid hormone increases catecholamine sensitivity — hence the tremor, tachycardia, and the usefulness of a beta blocker.
Absent uptake in thyroiditisPreformed hormone is leaking from damaged follicles; no new synthesis, so no iodine trapping.

Stalk effect on prolactinProlactin is under tonic dopamine inhibition; interrupt the stalk and the brake comes off.
Hyperpigmentation in Nelson syndromeAdrenalectomy removes cortisol feedback → ACTH rises enormously → pigmentary effects.
Sheehan syndromeThe pituitary enlarges in pregnancy while its blood supply does not; peripartum hemorrhage tips it into infarction.
Failure to suppress GH after oral glucoseHyperglycemia normally suppresses GH; an autonomous somatotroph adenoma ignores it.
GH rise on the insulin tolerance testHypoglycemia is a physiological stimulus to GH; failure to rise defines the deficiency.
No peripheral edema in SIADHWater is retained but blood volume stays normal, so it distributes rather than pooling in the interstitium.
Weight loss with polyphagiaCells cannot access glucose, so fat and protein are dismantled for fuel while the patient keeps eating.
Kussmaul respirationsRespiratory compensation for a metabolic acidosis — deep, rapid breathing drives PaCO₂ down.
Potassium paradox in DKAAcidosis and insulin deficiency push K⁺ out of cells, so serum K⁺ looks normal or high while total body K⁺ is depleted. Insulin then drops it fast.
No ketones in HHSSuppressing lipolysis takes less insulin than driving glucose into cells; enough remains for the first job, not the second.
Acanthosis nigricansA visible cutaneous marker of insulin resistance and hyperinsulinemia.

Proliferative retinopathyIschemia drives neovascularization; the new vessels are fragile and bleed.
Complications present at diagnosis of T2DMGlucose is the last variable to move — resistance and beta-cell decline preceded it by years.

Rapid recall prompts

Answer out loud before opening any of these.

GH → LH/FSH → TSH → ACTH. ACTH is the dangerous one: it is lost last, so it is easy to miss, and cortisol deficiency is what kills under the stress of illness or surgery. GH going first is useful in the other direction — its loss foreshadows the deficits still to come. See hypopituitarism.

Damaged follicles release hormone they made earlier. The gland is not synthesizing, so the uptake scan is blank and antithyroid drugs — which block synthesis — have nothing to act on. Symptom control with a beta blocker while it runs its course is the logical approach. See the thyroiditides.

Total body potassium is severely depleted — hours to days of osmotic diuresis, plus vomiting, have been washing it out. The serum value looks reassuring only because acidosis and insulin deficiency have shifted potassium out of cells into the compartment you are sampling. Start insulin and potassium moves back into cells; correcting the acidosis does the same. The serum concentration can fall quickly, unmasking the deficit. Hence close monitoring and anticipatory replacement. See DKA.

"Evaluate the insulin that acted before the repeated abnormal pattern."

Before — work backwards from the abnormal reading to whichever insulin was acting in the hours leading up to it, which is frequently not the dose given nearest in time. Repeated — a single bad day is a missed bolus or an unusual meal, not a pattern, and changing a standing dose on the strength of it causes hypoglycemia on all the normal days. Practice it in the pattern lab.

Suppressing lipolysis requires less insulin than driving glucose into cells does. In HHS there is just enough insulin to keep lipolysis switched off but not enough to support glucose utilization, so the glucose climbs enormously without ketones. In DKA insulin is essentially absent, lipolysis is released, and the liver converts the resulting free fatty acids into ketone bodies. One axis, two thresholds. See HHS versus DKA.

Step 1 — water deprivation. Ask whether they can concentrate their urine when dehydration should be a maximal stimulus to do so. Continuing to pass high volumes of dilute urine despite dehydration confirms an AVP disorder, without yet saying which.

Step 2 — give desmopressin. Now ask whether the hormone is missing or the kidney is deaf to it. Urine osmolality rises substantially → AVP deficiency (the kidney was competent, just unsupplied). Little or no rise → AVP resistance (the kidney cannot respond to hormone it is definitely now receiving). Serum AVP answers the same question from the other side: low in deficiency, normal or high in resistance. Work it interactively at the desmopressin figure.
